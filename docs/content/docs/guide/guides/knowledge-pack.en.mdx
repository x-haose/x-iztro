*For: anyone who wants written interpretation on top of a chart*

Casting a chart gives you facts: the Soul palace sits at Wu, Wuqu is in the Wealth palace carrying
the Quan transformation, this chart forms 府相朝垣. The next questions — "what does Wuqu mean",
"what is good about 府相朝垣" — are not facts. They are **opinions**, and different schools,
different books and different teachers answer them differently.

x-iztro keeps the two apart. The core only judges facts (charting, horoscopes, patterns); reading
texts and the school-specific star attributes live in a **knowledge pack**. A pack is a JSON file
whose protocol is "language-independent key → text and attributes". One default pack ships inside
the library so everything works out of the box; if you disagree with what it says, write an overlay
pack and change those entries. A pack is a parameter of the to_text family: hand it to
[text with readings](/en/docs/guide/guides/to-text#text-with-readings) and the readings, picked by
chart, are inlined next to the facts — each palace's star readings after that palace's fact lines,
the pattern readings after the pattern list, and the mutagen notes at the end.

## What belongs where

| | Core | Knowledge pack |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Contents | Twelve palaces, star placement, brightness, transformations, horoscopes, pattern hits | Star readings, pattern readings, palace and transformation meanings, glossary, school-specific star attributes |
| Nature | Facts, checkable field by field against iztro | Opinions; another school means another pack |
| What being wrong looks like | The chart is miscast | You disagree with the reading |
| How to change it | You cannot (changing it means it is no longer this algorithm) | Swap the pack or write an overlay |

The seam between them is the **language-independent key**: `ziweiMaj` for a star,
`zi_fu_tong_gong` for a pattern, `soulPalace` for a palace, `sihuaLu` for a transformation. Every
field the core emits carries these keys (see [the key contract](/en/docs/guide/guides/keys)), so you
take a key straight to the pack — no matching on translated names, and the chart language never
enters into it.

## What the bundled default pack contains

| Section | Entries | Contents |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `stars` | 164 | 14 major, 14 minor, 40 adjective, 46 decorative and 50 flowing stars — every `StarKey` has an entry. The major, minor, adjective and decorative ones carry the card attributes (yin-yang, five elements, dipper, chemistry, career, duty, aliases, element colour, energy colour) plus a body of text; the 14 major stars also carry readings for pairing with each other major star; the flowing-star entries (`category: "flow"`) are cross-references pointing at their natal minor-star counterparts, with the machine-readable table served by `flow_star_counterparts` (Go `FlowStarCounterparts`) |
| `patterns` | 64 | Classical quotations, a prose description of the conditions, and the reading |
| `palaces` | 12 | What each of the twelve palaces means |
| `mutagens` | 4 | What Lu, Quan, Ke and Ji each mean |
| `concepts` | 51 | Glossary and basic concepts (same palace, the palace in question, the Body palace, six-harmony branches, the surrounded set, flying-star transformations, brightness …) |
| `topics` | 13 | Reading topics: which palaces each kind of question looks at — see [Topics](#topics) |

The content comes from the 学习 (Learn) pages of
[iztro-docs](https://github.com/SylarLong/iztro-docs) (MIT License, by Sylar Long), pinned to a
source commit; the pack's `source` section records the origin, commit, licence and author in full.
The text has been edited by x-iztro into third-person reference prose (declared in
`source.adapted`). Text fields are Markdown. The 134 longer bodies (28 major and minor stars,
48 pairings, 43 patterns, 11 palaces, 4 transformations) also carry a [summary](#summaries) of at most
50 Chinese characters.

<Callout type="warn" title="The default pack is zh-CN only">
There is no bundled pack for the other five languages: Rust's `KnowledgePack::builtin` returns
`None`, Python, Go and JavaScript raise `invalid_argument`. For another language, write your own pack — or hand
the Chinese entries to an LLM along with the chart and let it translate as it interprets.
</Callout>

<Callout type="info">
The default pack adds roughly 380 KB to the wasm binary embedded in the Go package. The Rust and
Python sides embed the same data.
</Callout>

## Summaries

A body can carry a summary: `intro` pairs with `summary`, and every entry in `combinations` pairs with
the same key in `combinationSummaries`. Text with readings uses the summaries in place of the bodies
at the summary layer, bringing one chart's readings down from tens of thousands of characters to a
few thousand.

- Length is measured in display width: CJK characters, kana, hangul and full-width punctuation count
  2, every other character 1;
- A body no wider than 240 (120 Chinese characters) is already summary-sized; the summary layer uses
  it as is, and no separate summary is written;
- A longer body needs a summary: one paragraph, no wider than 100 (50 Chinese characters), condensing
  the original without adding or removing content;
- A pattern's `conditions` stay out of the summary layer.

If a pack has even one body wider than 240 without a summary, asking for the summary layer fails and
lists every missing entry — nothing is truncated, and the full text is never substituted. A pack you
write supports the summary layer once it follows these rules.

## Topics

Which palaces to read for romance, which for wealth — that is a school's method and schools differ,
so topics live in the pack too (the `topics` section); the core only selects palaces by the
definition and writes the [topic text](/en/docs/guide/guides/to-text#text-by-topic). A topic has two
palace groups: those in `surround` are taken together with their trine & opposite, those in
`palaces` on their own (`bodyPalace` and `originalPalace` are allowed).

```json
"topics": {
  "romance": {
    "title": "婚恋",
    "order": 50,
    "surround": ["spousePalace"],
    "palaces": ["soulPalace", "bodyPalace", "childrenPalace"],
    "intro": "看婚恋：夫妻宫是择偶观与对感情的态度，其三方四正（官禄、迁移、福德）一并会照；命宫、身宫看本人，子女宫是桃花宫位。"
  }
}
```

The default pack's 13 topics:

| `order` | slug | Topic | With trine & opposite | Palace only |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 10 | `overall` | 总论 overall | soul | body, spirit |
| 20 | `personality` | 性格 personality | — | soul, surface, spirit, body |
| 30 | `career` | 事业 career | career | — |
| 40 | `wealth` | 财运 wealth | wealth | property |
| 50 | `romance` | 婚恋 romance | spouse | soul, body, children |
| 60 | `health` | 健康 health | health | soul |
| 70 | `parents` | 父母 parents | parents | siblings, soul |
| 80 | `children` | 子女 children | children | soul |
| 90 | `siblings` | 兄弟 siblings | siblings | soul |
| 100 | `friends` | 交友 friends | friends | soul |
| 110 | `property` | 田宅 property | property | wealth |
| 120 | `travel` | 迁移 travel | surface | — |
| 130 | `spirit` | 精神 spirit | spirit | soul, body |

The choices follow the default pack's palace readings: accumulated wealth is read in the property
palace, the relationship with the mother in the siblings palace, romance in the children palace.
Topics read the natal palaces; questions about a period use the horoscope text. To disagree with a
topic's palaces, rewrite `surround` / `palaces` as a whole in an overlay (arrays are replaced
wholesale), or add topics of your own.

`order` sets the display order of topics: ascending `order`, topics without one last, ties and
topics without one by slug; the order in which keys are written in the JSON does not count. The
calls that list every topic return them in this order: Rust `topics_in_order`, Python and
TypeScript `topics()`, Go `TopicSlugs`. The default pack numbers its topics in steps of 10, so an
overlay that gives a new topic `"order": 35` places it between career and wealth; an overlay can also
move an existing topic by changing just its `order`.

## Picking by chart

The full pack holds 164 stars and 64 patterns; one chart uses far fewer. `for_astrolabe` cuts a
sub-pack containing only what is on this chart, and `for_horoscope` adds the horoscope scopes'
material on top:

| Section | `for_astrolabe` | `for_horoscope` adds |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `stars` | The stars on the twelve palaces: major, minor, adjective and the four groups of twelve gods; the 14 major stars' `combinations` keep only partners that actually share the palace | The flowing stars of each scope from the decadal down to the query precision |
| `patterns` | Patterns hit under the pattern criteria | Patterns hit from the decadal perspective down to the query precision |
| `mutagens` | All four entries Lu, Quan, Ke, Ji, independent of the chart | — |
| `palaces` / `concepts` | Empty — palaces and glossary do not depend on the chart; read them from the full pack as needed | — |

The horoscope scopes go only down to the precision of the horoscope's target: hourly for a date,
yearly for a lunar year, monthly for a lunar month — the finer scopes belong to a representative day
the kernel picked and stay out of the sub-pack. Every language takes the horoscope object and
follow its precision.

The pattern criteria are optional: Rust takes `for_astrolabe_with(&chart, &PatternConfig)` /
`for_horoscope_with(&h, &PatternConfig)` (the two without `_with` use the default),
Python a `config=` keyword, Go a second `*PatternConfig` argument (`nil` for the default). Pair the
sub-pack with `patterns` / `patterns_to_text` under the same criteria: different criteria hit
different sets, and the readings would then miss or over-include entries. Go also offers
`BuiltinKnowledge().ForAstrolabe(chart, nil)`, which lets the core read the bundled pack directly
instead of fetching the whole pack and sending it back.

The result is still a standard knowledge pack (metadata carried over from the source pack): merge
it, serialise it, look up keys in it, or pass it back as the reading parameter of to_text. The
sub-pack cut from the default pack for the 2000-8-16 Yin-hour female chart: 108 stars / 1 pattern /
4 mutagens for the natal chart, about 75 KB serialised (the full pack is about 219 KB); the
2025-1-1 horoscope adds flowing stars and each scope's patterns for 158 stars / 16 patterns.

## Writing an overlay pack

An overlay is the same format, containing only the entries and fields you want to change, with
`extends` naming the pack it overlays. This one replaces Ziwei's reading and aliases and swaps out
the reading for 紫府同宫, leaving everything else untouched:

```json
{
  "schema": 1,
  "id": "my-school",
  "version": "2026-08-19",
  "language": "zh-CN",
  "extends": "iztro-docs",
  "stars": {
    "ziweiMaj": {
      "intro": "紫微在我这一派看来先看格局高低，再论性情。",
      "attributes": { "aliases": ["帝座"] }
    }
  },
  "patterns": {
    "zi_fu_tong_gong": { "intro": "紫府同宫，我只把它当作起点高，不当作福厚。" }
  }
}
```

Merging produces a new pack:

<Tabs items={['Rust', 'Python', 'Go', 'TypeScript']}>
<Tab value="Rust">

```rust
let ziwei = pack.star(StarKey::ZiweiMaj).unwrap();
println!("{:?} {:?} {:?}", ziwei.name, ziwei.attributes.aliases, ziwei.attributes.chemistry);
println!("{:?}", pack.pattern_intro(PatternKey::ZiFuTongGong));
```

</Tab>

<Callout type="info">
Merging is implemented once, in the Rust core. Python's and JavaScript's `merged` and Go's `Merged` all call into
it, so the merged result is byte-identical across the languages instead of each side
re-implementing the rules.
</Callout>

## Checking a pack

Before importing someone else's pack or publishing your own, `check` lists every place it departs from the spec:
each issue carries a level, a JSON path, a code and parameters, and all issues come back at once rather than
stopping at the first. Input that is not an object, or JSON that does not parse, comes back as an issue too, not as
an exception; only usage errors (a base that is not a `KnowledgePack`, values JSON cannot represent) throw.

<Tabs items={['Rust', 'Python', 'Go', 'JavaScript']}>
<Tab value="Rust">

```rust
use x_iztro::{KnowledgePack, Language};

let raw: serde_json::Value = serde_json::from_str(r#"{"schema": 1, "id": "my-school", "version": "1", "language": "zh-CN", "extends": "iztro-docs",
 "stars": {"ziweiMjj": {"intro": "本门认为……"}, "tianjiMaj": {"intor": "……"}}}"#)?;
let base = KnowledgePack::builtin(Language::ZhCN).unwrap();
for issue in KnowledgePack::check(&raw, Some(base)) {
    println!("{} {issue}", issue.level.as_key());
}
```

```text
warning stars.tianjiMaj.intor: unknown field, dropped when parsed (did you mean 'intro'?)
error stars.ziweiMjj: unknown key 'ziweiMjj' (did you mean 'ziweiMaj'?)
```

</Tab>
<Tab value="Python">

The fields of an issue:

| Field | Description |
| --- | --- |
| `level` | `error` or `warning`, see below |
| `path` | JSON path: object keys as strings, array indexes as numbers; an empty path is the pack itself |
| `code` | The kind of issue, see the table below |
| `params` | Extra data whose keys depend on `code`; `suggestion` is the closest known key, see below |
| `message` | An English description with the dotted path, for logs; build UI text from `code` and `params` |

With a base, the base-relative reminders are reported too (an empty string overriding a base value, a language or
`extends` that does not match the base), and the summary rules judge each entry as it will be after merging onto
the base: an overlay that replaces a long body without a summary makes the base's old summary stale in the merge,
so it is reported as missing a summary. Without a base, a pack that declares `extends` cannot tell on its own
whether bodies and summaries pair up or which palaces a topic takes, so those two kinds are not reported.

### Levels and construction

- **error**: breaks a "must / must not" of the spec, or makes the core fail where it uses that spot. The pack
  constructors (Rust `from_json` / `from_value`, Python `from_dict` / `from_json`, Go `ParseKnowledgePack`,
  JavaScript `fromObject` / `fromJSON`, and overlay objects passed to `merged`) reject a pack with any error-level
  issue as `invalid_argument`, with every error-level issue in the message; the Python and JavaScript exceptions and
  Go's `*Error` also carry `issues`, the full list.
- **warning**: "should / should not", fields dropped when parsed, literal-match heuristics, and base-relative
  reminders. Reported, never rejected.

### Issue codes

§ in the Source column refers to `knowledge/SCHEMA.md` in the repository; "default-pack test" refers to the
assertions `tests/knowledge_pack.rs` makes about the bundled default pack. `suggestion?` means it may be absent.

| code | Level | Meaning | Source | params |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `invalid_json` | error | The text is not valid JSON (only entry points that take text report it) | §7 | `message` |
| `not_object` | error | The pack or an entry is not a JSON object | §2 | — |
| `schema_missing` | error | `schema` is missing, null or 0 | §2, §7 "must be rejected" | — |
| `schema_unsupported` | error | `schema` is newer than this library supports | §7 | `value`, `supported` |
| `wrong_type` | error | A field has the wrong type | Types in the §2 and §4 tables | `expected` |
| `required_missing` | error | `id` / `version` / `language` is missing or empty | §2 "required" | — |
| `unknown_language` | error | `language` is not an x-iztro language code | §2 | `value`, `suggestion`? |
| `unknown_key` | error | An entry key (including a combination's other star) is not a key the core knows | §3; the default-pack test asserts every key | `key`, `suggestion`? |
| `combination_key_not_major` | error | A combination's other star is a star key but not a major star | §4.1 "keyed by the other major star" | `key` |
| `invalid_value` | error | `category`, `yinYang`, `fiveElements` or `stem` is outside its allowed set | Values in §4.1 and §4.1.1 | `value`, `allowed`, `suggestion`? |

| `image_in_text` | error | Text contains a Markdown image | §5 "must not use images" | `snippet` |
| `external_link` | error | Text contains an `http(s)://` URL or a `](//` link (bare URLs render as links too) | §5 "must not use external links" | `url` |
| `unknown_field` | warning | A field outside the spec, dropped when parsed | §2, §7 implementation behaviour: dropped | `suggestion`? |
| `empty_string` | warning | A field is an empty string | §4 "must not express absence with an empty string" | — |
| `summary_missing` | warning | Body wider than 240 with no summary (the summary layer fails on it) | §5.1 "should" have a summary | `width`, `limit` |
| `summary_not_needed` | warning | Body at most 240 wide but a summary is given | §5.1 "should not" add a summary | `width`, `limit` |
| `summary_too_wide` | warning | Summary wider than 100 | §5.1 "should" be at most 100 | `width`, `limit` |
| `summary_multi_paragraph` | warning | Summary is not a single paragraph | §5.1 "should be one paragraph" | — |
| `summary_without_body` | warning | A summary has no matching body | §5.1 bodies and summaries pair; default-pack test | — |
| `page_reference` | warning | Text contains a page reference such as 见上表, 详见, 点击 | §5 "must not rely on page context" (literal match) | `phrase` |
| `topic_without_palaces` | warning | A topic selects no palace | Default-pack test: every topic selects a palace | — |
| `overrides_with_empty` | warning | An empty string overrides a base value (only with a base) | §4, §6.1: an empty string counts and overrides | `base` |
| `language_differs_from_base` | warning | Language differs from the base pack (only with a base) | §6.2: the merge takes the overlay's language | `value`, `base` |
| `extends_differs_from_base` | warning | `extends` differs from the base pack's `id` (only with a base) | §2: `extends` names the base pack's `id` | `value`, `base` |

HTML, images, external links and page references are checked only in the text fields SCHEMA §5 lists (each entry's
`intro`, `summary`, `conditions`, and the values of both combination maps), and nothing inside inline code counts (per CommonMark: an escaped backtick opens no code span, and a span does not cross a blank line);
the classical quotations in `quotes` are verbatim plain text and are not checked. The bundled default pack checks clean.

### The closest key

`unknown_key`, `unknown_language`, `unknown_palace`, `invalid_value` and `unknown_field` give a `suggestion` when
one is found: first a candidate differing only in case (language codes also accept underscores, so `zh_cn` suggests
`zh-CN`); otherwise the candidate at the smallest edit distance. Insertions, deletions, substitutions and swaps of two
adjacent characters each count 1 (a swap is the commonest slip in typing a key; `ziwieMaj` is 1 away from
`ziweiMaj`), capped at a third of the misspelt word's length (counting at least 3), the same threshold rustc uses when
it suggests a name; beyond the cap no suggestion is given, rather than forcing an unrelated word. Ties go to the key
listed first.

### Only part of the tone rules applies

The default pack has a tone test that bans phrasings such as 读者, 大家肯定, 韭菜, 见上表 and 点击. The part that
points at page layout or site features (见上表, 下表中, 前面提到, 详见, 参见, 本站, 点击 …) falls under §5 "must not
rely on page context", which holds for any pack; `page_reference` checks only that part, by literal match, as a
warning. The rest — colloquialisms and conversational leftovers — is the default pack's own "third-person reference
voice" (§8); the spec does not ask user packs to adopt it, so it is not checked.

## Merge rules

Starting from the base pack, each section (`stars`, `patterns`, `palaces`, `mutagens`, `concepts`,
`topics`) is merged key by key:

- For an entry present in the overlay, its **non-null fields** replace the corresponding fields of
  the same-keyed base entry; fields it does not mention are kept
- `attributes` and `combinations` merge the same way, field by field and sub-key by sub-key
- Array fields (`aliases`, `quotes`, a topic's `surround` / `palaces`) are replaced wholesale, never
  merged element by element
- Keys the base does not have are added
- Writing a field explicitly as `null` does **not** delete the base content (absent and null mean
  the same thing); to delete, replace the whole pack
- The one case that removes content: when an overlay entry replaces `intro` (or a pairing reading)
  without supplying the matching summary, the base's old summary is dropped — it describes the text
  that was replaced, and keeping it would make the summary layer speak for the old school
- After merging, `id`, `version`, `language` and `source` come from the overlay when non-empty,
  while `extends` stays the base's

A `schema` newer than this library supports is an error rather than a best-effort parse.

## Why the star attributes live here

A star's five-element attribution looks like a fact but is also an opinion. iztro's own `starsInfo`
table and the iztro-docs star cards already disagree with each other:

| Star | iztro `starsInfo` | iztro-docs card |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 贪狼 (Tanlang) | Water | 甲 (Jia) Wood — qi is Water |
| 巨门 (Jumen) | yin Earth | 癸 (Gui) Water and 己 (Ji) Earth (holding Metal and Wood) |

Two data sets by the same author disagreeing is the clearest sign that these attributes are a
school's reading, not a single answer. So x-iztro's core `StarInfo` stays value-for-value identical
to iztro's (code ported from it does not change behaviour), while the card attributes go into the
knowledge pack where they can be swapped.
